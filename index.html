Operating standards

How I run myself and the team. Break one of these and the call is wrong even when the spreadsheet looks fine.

Relentlessly pursue purposeful progress

Busy is not progress. Every report, system, or automation has to move the business in a way you can measure and defend.

Give more in value than you receive in compensation

Be early with clarity. Surface problems before they get expensive. Recommend what serves the whole company, not the silo.

Everyone sweeps the floor

No task is beneath the person who owns the outcome. Titles rotate. Standards don't. If you won't do the work, you can't judge it.

Share good news quickly, and bad news even quicker

Surprises cost the most. The sooner the right people hear it, the smaller the fix. That's respect for capital and for people.

Treat every experience as an opportunity to learn

Stay a student. When the variance is good or bad, the useful question is: what did we see too late, and how do we see it sooner next time?

Outlook

Closing the books is table stakes. The edge is data, less re-keying, and capital aimed at the few constraints that move the P&L.

Common default

Reporting CFO

  • Monthly close is the main event
  • Spreadsheet is the system of record
  • You explain the variance after it happened
  • Finance keeps score
  • Tools live with IT or a vendor

My seat

How I work

  • Plant and ERP signals show up in time to act
  • One shared model everyone uses
  • Forecasts move when the plants move
  • Finance builds the path to the decision
  • I can build and defend the systems myself

What's already moving

  • AI agents taking the repeat work in finance and accounting
  • Plant and ERP data shortening the close
  • Spreadsheets as a view, not the books
  • CFOs expected to build the pipes, not only read the P&L

Where I'm putting time

  • Agents on real plant and finance data, not demos
  • Direct Lake models as the shared way into the numbers
  • Natural-language questions over ops for operators and finance
  • Smaller connected systems instead of one finance monolith

What can break it

  • ERP lock-in and brittle integrations that block a clean model
  • Bad plant data: wrong signal, wrong capital call
  • Teams that can close the books but can't run the systems
  • Short hold periods and tight capital that punish multi-year builds
