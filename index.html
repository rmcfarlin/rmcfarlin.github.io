What we built

The finance team runs on these today.

  • Reporting. We rebuilt company reporting on a Microsoft Fabric lakehouse with a Direct Lake semantic model. Production, labor, and financial data now update in close to real time instead of once a month.
  • Capital approval. Big spending requests go through stage gates, the way a private equity firm would run them, and we forecast fixed charge coverage across the whole portfolio before we commit.
  • Automation. The recurring manual work is automated, so the team spends that time on analysis.
  • Internal audit. As the company grew it needed someone looking at governance, compliance, and risk. We built that function from nothing.
  • One HR system. Four locations used to run payroll and HR separately. They now share one HRIS.

Approach

How I think about the job

Most of what I do comes back to one of these questions.

Strategy

Where should we compete?

Put capital into the few things that reinforce each other, and stop funding the rest.

Focus

What actually makes us money?

Find the customers, products, plants, and workflows that drive most of the profit, cash, and risk. It’s usually a short list.

Reporting

How soon can we know?

A month-end package tells you what already happened. I’d rather have operating data every day, forecasts that update quickly, and controls built into the work itself.

Principles

Five rules I work by

How I run a finance team, and what the people around me can expect.

  1. 01

    Make progress you can measure

    Every system, report, or automation has to move the business forward in a way I can measure and defend.

  2. 02

    Give more in value than you receive in compensation

    Deliver clear answers early, raise problems before they get expensive, and work for the whole company.

  3. 03

    Everyone sweeps the floor

    No task is beneath the person accountable for the result.

  4. 04

    Share good news quickly, and bad news even quicker

    The sooner the people who can act hear about a problem, the smaller the fix.

  5. 05

    Treat every experience as a chance to learn

    When something goes wrong, ask what we saw too late and how to see it sooner next time.

Outlook

Where the CFO role is going

I expect the most valuable CFOs of the next decade to own the data systems that produce the forecast.

Happening now

What’s already changing

  • AI agents doing routine accounting and finance work
  • Daily operating data shortening the monthly close
  • Spreadsheets used for analysis instead of as the system of record
  • CFOs owning data systems as well as the books
In progress at RCM

What I’m building

  • AI agents that answer financial and operating questions
  • Direct Lake semantic models as the main way people read the general ledger
  • Plain-English questions as the query interface for operations
  • Modular finance systems in place of one large ERP
